# Team BSD: technical document

More than a Hack 2027, Phase 1 (FC Barcelona `H` vs Athletic Club `A`). Submission file `BSD.csv`,
SHA-256 `aaf42ebbf0d41f0285877869f1fda9e4d5ee51068a98713b204ee981a4c3b450`, regenerated by
`python -m bsd.run` (see [REPRODUCE.md](../REPRODUCE.md)). All code is in `bsd/`; module docstrings
give the details behind every section below.

## 1. Summary

A per-frame classifier gives evidence for `H` / `A` / `D` on every frame, and a Viterbi decoder
turns that evidence into stable possession runs. The classifier is a gradient-boosted tree model
trained on Metrica Sports' three public sample games. Their tracking is first degraded to look
like this match's automatic tracking, and their labels are derived by code from Metrica's event
data. A second model of the same kind detects hand-overs and sets a per-frame H<->A switch cost.
The decoder's transition costs and the cost schedule were tuned for spellF1 on a leave-one-game-out
bench over those sample games. The match's Smart Tagging adds small fixed-weight evidence. No number
was fitted to this match's labels.

| | spellF1 | macroF1 |
|---|---|---|
| Sample-game bench, hand-over features + detector (adopted, v2) | 0.5976 | 0.8480 |
| Sample-game bench, learned evidence only (shipped v1) | 0.5440 | 0.8372 |
| Sample-game bench, hand-set heuristic evidence | 0.2567 | 0.5006 |

Bench figures are leave-one-game-out means over 6 held-out cases (3 games × 2 degradation seeds),
with decoder costs tuned in sample (see §7). The heuristic row uses hand-set evidence with
decoder costs tuned the same way. The published floor on the full match (`baseline_smooth50`) is
spellF1 0.2433, but it is not directly comparable: it is a different game with different
tracking. On BAR-ATH the submission has 223 possessions, mean
18.8 s, and a dead-ball share of 34.8 % / 33.3 % per half (shipped v1: 256, 16.3 s, 34.8 % / 33.8 %).

## 2. Approach, and why

spellF1 rewards whole possessions with the right boundaries. Two things decide it: alive/dead
boundaries (every stoppage splits a possession) and hand-overs (one flickering frame creates two
false possessions). That favours a **sequence model**: per-frame evidence, then a decoder that
changes the label only when the evidence for the change outweighs a cost.

Options we considered:

| approach | why not / why |
|---|---|
| Hand-written state machine over tracking and tagging | Needs dozens of thresholds that cannot legally be tuned on this match. Our hand-set version (`bsd/heuristic.py`) is kept as a baseline and loses clearly on the bench (0.2567 vs 0.5440 for v1 evidence). |
| Our own ball/player detection from video | Hours per run and no way to measure its payoff before the deadline. |
| Copying Smart Tagging intervals as labels | Tagging intervals are phases, not possessions, and start or end seconds away from the GT boundaries. |
| **Hybrid HMM: learned per-frame evidence + Viterbi over {H, A, D}** | Fits spellF1's segment view, treats a missing ball as weak evidence rather than a failure, and has only 3 decoder parameters to tune. Labelled data from other games gives a legitimate training and validation set. **Chosen.** |

### Pipeline

1. **Tracking** (`bsd/io_epts.py`). A streaming parser for the FIFA/EPTS ATD gives player
   positions per slot, slot team (from the XML), and the ball, in metres (105.3 × 68.0). Row `i`
   = `frame_id − 1` = video frame `i`. The decode windows are the two graded halves from
   `games.half_video_blocks("BAR-ATH")`, published game constants. Every frame outside them is
   written `X`.
2. **Features** (`bsd/features.py`, 50 columns). Slot-agnostic team quantities, because slots are
   tracklets, not identities:
   - Ball: visible, out of play (0.5 m margin), signed distance to the nearest line, speed from
     positions (the ATD's speed field is unreliable), static, frames since last seen.
   - Players near the ball: nearest home/away player distance, counts within 2 m and 5 m.
   - Each team: centroid depth and width in its own attacking frame, mean forward velocity,
     tracked count.
   - Pace: overall player speed and its trend.
   - Context: 1 s and 3 s rolling context of the key columns.

   Attacking direction is inferred per period from mean team positions. Slot jumps faster than
   12 m/s are treated as identity swaps.
3. **Degradation of the training games** (`bsd/degrade.py`). Sample-game tracking is clean, so
   before training it is made to look like BAR-ATH:
   - The ball is hidden with alternating visible/missing runs resampled from BAR-ATH's own
     run-length distribution (a label-free statistic, about 41 % missing).
   - The ball is never invented where the source has none.
   - Player observations are dropped to BAR-ATH's tracked-count distribution.
   - Smooth position jitter (σ ≈ 0.3 m) is added.
   - Slot identities are shuffled in 10–60 s blocks.

   Everything is seeded.
4. **Per-frame classifier** (`bsd/model.py`). `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` from scikit-learn:
   150 rounds, 31 leaves, at least 100 samples per leaf, L2 1.0, no early stopping,
   `random_state=0`, one thread.
   - Training rows: every 3rd labelled frame, each frame both as is and mirrored (home/away
     swapped, H/A labels swapped).
   - The prediction is averaged with the swapped prediction of the mirrored frame, so the
     evidence is exactly team-symmetric.
   - Rows are reweighted so that the ball is visible on 59.3 % of each label's rows, BAR-ATH's
     in-period rate. In the sample games the ball is missing on 92–99.7 % of dead frames, and
     without this the model would learn "no ball ⇒ dead", which is false here: a missing ball on
     BAR-ATH is mostly occlusion.
   - Evidence = `log(p / prior)`, rounded to 6 decimals.
5. **Smart Tagging** (`bsd/tagging.py`). Fixed-weight log-score bonuses; see §5.
6. **Decoder** (`bsd/decode.py`). Viterbi over {H, A, D}, run independently on each graded half,
   maximising Σ evidence − Σ transition costs. Costs (nats, from `bsd/decode_params.json`):
   - `H↔A` switch: per frame, see step 6a (base 10)
   - alive → dead: 40
   - dead → alive: 80

   6a. **Hand-over features and detector** (`bsd/handover.py`, `bsd/decode.py`). The frame features
   gain extra hand-over columns (`frame_features(handover=True)`). A second
   `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` (same settings, mirrored training rows and visibility
   weights as the emitter) is trained on the same degraded sample games to predict "an H<->A
   change occurs within 0.5 s of this frame" (labels from `change_labels`, derived from the
   event-based labels). Its probability `p` sets the H<->A cost of frame `t`:
   `max(0.5, 10 - 16 * logit(p_t))`. A likely hand-over is cheap to decode, an unlikely one costs
   up to 10 + 16 * 9.2 nats. Abstaining rows (NaN) use the base cost 10.
7. **Output** (`bsd/output.py`, `bsd/run.py`). Writes `evaluation/results/BSD.csv` (LF line
   endings, so the bytes are a function of the labels), runs the official validator on it, and
   prints label-free diagnostics. No `X` is written inside the graded halves.

## 3. Assets used and dropped

| asset | used? | how / why not |
|---|---|---|
| Tactical ATD (tracking incl. ball, `*_atd.txt` + `.xml`) | **yes** | All per-frame features. Also the label-free defect statistics (ball gap lengths, missing rate, tracked-player counts) that the training games are degraded to match. |
| Tactical Smart Tagging (`*_smart_tagging.xml`) | **yes** | Small fixed-weight evidence (§5). |
| Tactical video | dropped | Our own ball/player detection could not be built and validated before the deadline, so we used the provided tracking. |
| Tactical homography | dropped | Only needed to project video detections onto the pitch. The ATD is already in pitch coordinates. |
| Broadcast video, ATD and homography | dropped | On its own unsynchronised timeline (164,488 frames). Using it would first mean aligning it to the graded tactical frames, which was not worth it before the deadline. |
| Public 5-minute GT | read only | Read to understand the label conventions (§5, `docs/notes/label-conventions.md`) and scored once at the end as a correctness check (§8). Nothing was tuned on it. |

## 4. Training data

The only labelled data are the **Metrica Sports public sample data**,
<https://github.com/metrica-sports/sample-data>, pinned at commit
`e706dd506b360d69d9d123d5b8026e7294b13996` and downloaded by `python -m bsd.fetch_sample`. We
thank Metrica Sports for publishing it.

- **What**: three anonymised professional matches with tracking (players and ball, 25 fps) and
  event data. Games 1–2 are in Metrica CSV format; game 3 is in FIFA/EPTS tracking with JSON
  events. None of it is about the provided game (RULES.md allows human-labelled data from other
  games).
- **Who labelled it**: the event data were produced by Metrica Sports (1,745, 1,935 and 3,620
  events in games 1–3). We did not hand-label anything.
- **Our labels**: per-frame H/A/D labels are derived from those events by code
  (`bsd/events.py`, rules in §5). No person chose any label.
- **How much**: 428,169 labelled frames (about 4.8 h; per game 96, 94 and 95 min). The final model
  sees every game degraded with 3 seeds (100, 101, 102), every 3rd frame, plus mirrored copies.
- **Provided game**: no hand labels, no event data, no corrected tracking, and no rules or
  constants tied to specific frames of BAR-ATH (a unit test, `tests/test_no_literals.py`, scans
  `bsd/` for frame-like numbers). A generic Smart Tagging rule (§5) makes the second-half
  pre-kickoff stretch mostly `D` (about 73 % of its frames); it is not a hard-coded frame, and
  nothing in the model was changed because of it. Short spurious H/A spells remain there (§9).

## 5. Possession rules

### Label definition (training labels, `bsd/events.py`)

These rules follow CHALLENGE.md and were checked qualitatively against the public window
(`docs/notes/label-conventions.md`). No rule was changed after that check.

- **Owner.** Each possession-establishing event (`PASS`, `CARRY`, `RECOVERY`, `SHOT`,
  `SET PIECE`) sets the owner from its start frame. `BALL LOST` and challenges without control
  do not switch possession, so a loose or contested ball stays with the last team. A short
  control by the other team (a recovery, however brief) is a possession, as in the GT window.
- **Hand-over.** The new team owns from its own first control, not from the losing team's last
  pass.
- **Dead spell start.**
  - Ball out of play: at the end frame of the `BALL OUT` event (in the sample games that is the
    first frame the ball is beyond the line).
  - Fouls (`FAULT RECEIVED`, a foul `CHALLENGE`): at the whistle, even while the ball still
    moves.
  - Shots that go out, and goals.
- **Dead spell end.** At the restart touch: the next `SET PIECE` or goal-kick event. If the next
  event is a control event and no restart was logged, the spell ends at that event. A foul
  followed directly by a control event is **advantage**: no dead spell at all. Events at the
  same frame as the trigger cannot end it (game 3 logs a carry at the ball-out frame).
- **Restarts.** The restarting team owns from the restart frame. There is no neutral stretch.
- **Kick-off.** The kick-off is alive and owned by the kicking team. Frames before the first
  owner event of a period are `X` (unscored) in the training labels.

### What the decoder does with it

The classifier learns these conventions from the labels. The decoder then only changes label
when the accumulated evidence pays the costs above. In practice:

- a loose ball or a one-touch duel does not switch possession unless the other team's evidence
  persists;
- a short stoppage must be clearly dead to be labelled `D` (entering and leaving costs 60 nats);
- after a restart, the label follows the evidence for the restarting team.

The costs were chosen only by mean held-out spellF1 on the sample-game bench.

### Smart Tagging rules (BAR-ATH only, fixed first-principles weights, `bsd/tagging.py`)

Codes and values are matched case-insensitively.

| rule | bonus |
|---|---|
| Set piece | +2.0 nats on D over the 3 s before its start (the ball is dead before a restart), and +1.0 on the tagged team over its first 2 s (nothing when `Team` is `N/A`, e.g. throw-ins) |
| Goal | +2.0 on D from the goal's end to the next kick-off set piece (capped at 90 s) |
| Other team-tagged phase | +0.3 on that team over its interval |
| Pre-kick-off | Per period, frames before the first `Kick Off (Start)` set piece that overlaps the period get +3.0 on D. No bonus if the tag starts at or before the period's first frame; `Kick Off (After Goal)` is never used. This makes the second-half warm-up stretch mostly `D` (about 73 % of its frames) without any literal frame; short spurious H/A spells remain (§9). |

These weights could not be validated, because the sample games have no Smart Tagging. With
tagging off the run gives 273 possessions (mean 15.35 s) instead of 256. We kept it on because the
change is modest and every rule follows directly from what a set piece, goal or kick-off means.

## 6. Determinism

Everything that could vary between runs or machines is fixed:

- every random draw is `np.random.default_rng(seed)` with an explicit seed;
- the classifier is single-threaded (`threadpoolctl`), with fixed iterations and no early
  stopping;
- probabilities are rounded to 1e-6 before decoding;
- Python and all packages, direct and indirect, are pinned (`requirements-bsd.txt`; the base
  `requirements.txt` it includes lists unpinned numpy and pandas, which the exact pins here fix);
- the sample data are pinned to a commit.

`python -m bsd.run --check-sha <hex>` fails if the output differs. The published SHA-256 was
produced twice with identical output (v1 was also verified from a fresh `git archive` checkout with
an empty cache) on macOS arm64 with Python 3.14.6. Byte-identity on other platforms is expected but
was not verified.

## 7. Validation (sample-game bench, `bsd/bench.py`, `docs/notes/bench-results.md`)

- **Folds.** Leave one game out. Each fold's classifier is trained on the other two games
  (degraded with seeds 100–102). It is tested on the held-out game degraded with seeds 0 and 1,
  scored with the official `evaluation/possession_metrics.py` functions.
- **Tuning.** The decoder costs are grid-searched for mean held-out spellF1:
  - `switch_ha` ∈ {5, …, 80}, `alive_to_dead` ∈ {5, …, 40}, `dead_to_alive` ∈ {5, …, 40};
  - an axis whose best value sits on the grid edge is extended once.

  Both emitters were tuned the same way, and the winner was adopted.
- **Results (v1).** The learned emitter wins on all 6 cases: spellF1 0.5440 vs 0.2567, macroF1
  0.8372 vs 0.5006. Its optimum is flat for `switch_ha` 10–40 (0.540–0.544).
- **Stage 1 (v2).** `python -m bsd.experiments stage1` compared four variants on the same 6 cases
  and folds (`bsd/experiments_stage1.json`, `docs/notes/bench-results.md`). The grid was
  `switch_ha` {10, 20, 40} x D pairs {(10, 20), (20, 40), (40, 80)} x `alpha` {0, 1, 2, 4, 8} x
  `floor` {1, 5}, with edge extension.

| variant | spellF1 | macroF1 | n poss | mean poss s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 (default features, constant costs) | 0.5440 | 0.8372 | 256.8 | 14.69 |
| hand-over features only | 0.5644 | 0.8421 | 262.8 | 14.32 |
| detector only | 0.5737 | 0.8442 | 233.7 | 16.15 |
| **hand-over features + detector (adopted)** | **0.5976** | **0.8480** | 259.0 | 14.55 |
| features + detector, 1 s window | 0.5903 | 0.8455 | 273.0 | 13.76 |

  The adopted variant beats v1 on all 6 cases (mean +0.054 spellF1, +0.011 macroF1). Adopted
  parameters: `switch_ha` 10, `alive_to_dead` 40, `dead_to_alive` 80, `alpha` 16, `floor` 0.5.

## 9. Limitations, and what we would do with more time

- **Bench numbers are optimistic.** The classifiers are trained out of fold, but the decoder
  costs were picked on the same held-out cases that are reported. The detector variants searched
  192-256 grid points against 9 for v1, so their in-sample selection bias is larger. The sample games are also
  cleaner and closer to their own labels than BAR-ATH is to its GT.
- **Edge-of-grid parameters.** The adopted point sits on edges: `alpha` 16 and `floor` 0.5 are
  once-extended edges (the curve is flat there: 0.5971 at alpha 8), and the D pair (40, 80) is the
  top of the unextended D axis. Inside the original grid (alpha <= 8, floor {1, 5}) the same
  variant reaches 0.5954 (+0.051 over v1), so the gain does not rest on the extensions.
- **Not re-checked on the public window.** §8 describes the v1 file. The v2 file was not scored
  against the public GT; no parameter was chosen with it.
- **`bsd.bench --mode learned` is the v1 protocol.** While the committed
  `bsd/decode_params.json` has a `handover` block it does not overwrite that file; it writes
  `cache/decode_params_v1protocol.json`. The Stage 1 parameters come from
  `python -m bsd.experiments stage1`. Rerunning either is not part of the reproduction.
- **Residual "ball missing ⇒ dead" cue.** In the degraded sample games the ball is visible on
  only 0.2–4.9 % of dead frames, so visibility weights for visible-dead rows are large (about
  12–300). The model still keeps a small part of the cue, which may label long BAR-ATH occlusions
  as dead. With more time: more varied visible-dead training data, or a model that conditions on
  visibility explicitly.
- **Under-segmentation.** We predict fewer, longer possessions than the event-derived labels (about
  257 vs 349 per sample game, 14.7 s vs 10.8 s), so short duel possessions and quick hand-overs
  are missed. Duration-aware states or a hand-over-specific cue (first touch by the new team)
  would help.
- **Smart Tagging weights are unvalidated** first-principles guesses (no tagging in the sample
  games).
- **Pre-kickoff stretch is only mostly dead.** The pre-kickoff rule (§5) makes the second-half
  pre-kickoff stretch mostly `D` (about 73 % of its frames), but short spurious H/A spells remain:
  the +3.0-nat prior is weaker than the learned model's strongest evidence, and the
  `Kick Off (Start)` tag begins a few seconds before the actual kickoff. Nothing in the model was
  changed because of this.
- **Phase-team bonus may cancel.** Smart Tagging phase codes come in pairs (each in-possession code
  has a matching "defending..." code tagged to the other team), so the +0.3 phase-team bonus
  largely cancels and may point the wrong way where the paired intervals do not coincide. Left
  unchanged and not tested on the bench.
- **Set-piece tags start early.** Set-piece tags start a few seconds before the restart, so the
  +1.0 team bonus over the tag's first 2 s can fall inside the dead spell.
- **Home team by name.** `bsd/tagging.py` maps tagging teams to H/A with the constant
  `HOME_NAME = "barcelona"`.
- **Decoder parametrisation.** `alive_to_dead` and `dead_to_alive` are nearly redundant: only
  their sum matters to first order, because every dead spell is entered and left once.
- **Label definition gap.** Our labels come from event logs; the GT is built from corrected
  tracking. Boundary timing (exact first-control frame, whistle frame) differs between the two.
- **Domain gap.** Three anonymous matches are a small training set, and degradation imitates
  BAR-ATH's defects statistically, not exactly. More labelled games, or self-training on
  BAR-ATH's own unlabelled frames, would be next.
- **No video.** Re-detecting the ball during the ~41 % of graded frames where it is missing is
  the largest untapped source of information.
